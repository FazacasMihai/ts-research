\section{Case study}\label{sec:case}

This section reports the results on the four datasets of
Table~\ref{tab:datasets}. The feature methods were run on the full grid of
Section~\ref{sec:design}. For the neural models there are three sets of
runs: a first, untuned check of all five models; the hyperparameter
search; and a test run of the tuned models, for four models on clean data
and for GRU, LSTM and CfC also with missing points. The full grid for the
Neural CDE and LTC with missing points was not run, because of its cost.

\subsection{Setup}

All methods are implemented in Python 3.14 with NumPy 2.3,
scikit-learn 1.9 \cite{pedregosa2011sklearn} and PyTorch 2.15; the
datasets are loaded with aeon 1.6, and the CfC and LTC cells come from
ncps 1.0.1. The signatures and DMD are our own NumPy code. The feature
methods and the first neural run were made on one laptop CPU (Intel Core
Ultra 7 155H, 32 GB of memory); the hyperparameter search and the tuned
runs were made on a GPU in Google Colab. All times are wall-clock times
and are only comparable between runs on the same machine. The results of
every run are stored in CSV files in the project repository.

Three implementation problems came up and are worth reporting, because
they stop the training or, worse, change the results without raising an
error.
\begin{itemize}
\item The sequence wrappers for CfC and LTC in ncps 1.0.1 reshape the
time gaps of a batch to a vector of length equal to the batch size before
multiplying them with the hidden state. This fails when the batch size
differs from the hidden size and is silently wrong when the two are equal.
We therefore loop over the cells ourselves and pass the gaps with shape
(batch, 1).
\item The LTC update divides by the step length (Section~\ref{sec:neural}),
so a gap of zero gives an invalid result; the first gap is therefore set
to at least one step.
\item With \texttt{torchcde} 0.2.5, padding a series (repeating its last
point) changed the output of the Neural CDE: the linear interpolation
takes the derivative at an observation from the segment before it, so the
state keeps moving after the last real point. We therefore solve the CDE
with our own Runge--Kutta steps, segment by segment.
\end{itemize}
Each of these is checked by an automatic test: a batch gives the same
output as one series at a time, and padding does not change the output.

Three observations stand out.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The depth-3 signature is the best method on three of the four
datasets, and it loses little accuracy when points are removed: from
$p = 0$ to $p = 0.7$ it drops by 0.052 on NATOPS and 0.080 on Libras, and
not at all on ArticularyWordRecognition. Since the signature works on the
path with its real times, a missing point only makes one segment of the
path longer. Depth 3 is clearly better than depth 2 on Libras (0.700
against 0.472), which suggests that the third level carries information
about the shape of the movement that the second level does not.
\item BasicMotions is too easy to separate the methods: the summary
statistics already reach 100\% on clean data. On Libras, with only two
channels, the summary statistics are almost as good as the depth-3
signature and slightly better at $p = 0.7$.
\item DMD with 8 features is much weaker than the other methods, except on
clean BasicMotions: 0.187 on ArticularyWordRecognition (25 classes) and 0.383 on
NATOPS. A likely reason is that a few growth rates and frequencies of one
linear model of the whole series do not describe the shape of a movement,
and that interpolating over gaps smooths away part of what is left; it
also drops the most with missing points on NATOPS and BasicMotions.
\end{enumerate}
The spread over seeds is at most 0.118 (DMD on BasicMotions, $p = 0.5$)
and below 0.06 in most cells, so differences of a few hundredths between
methods are not significant with three seeds.

In this first check, the only continuous-time model that beats both the
GRU and the LSTM is the Neural CDE on clean Libras (0.717), and it loses

the most accurate in the other three settings. CfC stopped early in every

than to a property of the models. These results motivated the equal
hyperparameter search below. They remain the only results for the Neural
CDE and LTC with missing points.

The Neural CDE was first solved with a single Runge--Kutta step per
segment, so removing points also removed solver steps. With that solver
its accuracy at $p = 0.5$ was 0.475 on BasicMotions and 0.472 on Libras.
With one step per original time step (Section~\ref{sec:neural}) it is
0.550 and 0.478, the same at $p = 0$. The coarser solver therefore
explains only a small part of the drop.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

The feature methods are cheap: computing the features and fitting the
classifier takes less than 0.4\,s on BasicMotions and Libras, and at most
1.9\,s on any dataset (depth-3 signature on NATOPS, with 16\,275
features). The neural models have between 14\,000 and 34\,000 trainable
parameters and are much slower to train (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). LTC is the
most expensive, about 80 times slower per epoch than the GRU on
BasicMotions, mainly because every step solves its ODE in six sub-steps. Note
that the size of the depth-3 signature grows with the cube of the number
of channels, while the size of the neural models grows mainly with $H$;
only the Neural CDE also grows with the channels (34\,052 parameters on
BasicMotions, 110\,214 on NATOPS).

The GPU runs show the same large differences. The 114 runs of the hyperparameter
search took 3.4 hours in total; a run took on average 2\,s for the GRU and
the LSTM, 68\,s for CfC, 204\,s for LTC (on the three smaller datasets) and
291\,s for the Neural CDE. In the tuned run on clean
ArticularyWordRecognition, the GRU trained in 1.4\,s and the Neural CDE in
1\,032\,s. The GRU and LSTM use the fused recurrent layers of PyTorch,
while CfC, LTC and the Neural CDE loop over the time steps in Python, with
several small operations per step (six solver steps for LTC and four
evaluations of $f_\theta$ for the Neural CDE).

\subsection{Discussion}

For RQ1, the continuous-time models are not better than the GRU and LSTM
on clean data in our setting. After an equal search, the GRU is the best
neural model on three of the four datasets; the Neural CDE is the best on
BasicMotions and close on two more, CfC is clearly behind, and LTC hardly
learned on Libras and NATOPS.

RQ2 is answered only in part. With missing points, the GRU with the time
gap as input stays within 0.072 of its clean accuracy on three datasets
and fails only on Libras; CfC shows no advantage. For the Neural CDE and
LTC there is only the untuned first run, in which the Neural CDE lost more
accuracy than the LSTM. We therefore found no evidence that the
continuous-time models handle missing data better, but the evidence for
the Neural CDE and LTC is weak.

For RQ3, the depth-3 signature with logistic regression gets close to the
networks and sometimes beats them. It is the best method of all on
ArticularyWordRecognition (0.980 against at most 0.897), much more robust
than the networks on Libras (at $p = 0.7$ it loses 0.080, the GRU 0.326),
and 0.050 below the GRU on clean NATOPS. It is also cheaper: 0.17\,s on
BasicMotions against 6\,s for the GRU and 82\,s for the Neural CDE on the
same CPU (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). On BasicMotions the summary statistics
are better than every network.

The case study has clear limitations. The datasets are small (40 to 275
training series), so a single wrong prediction moves the accuracy by up to
2.5\%, and three seeds give only a rough idea of the spread. Points are
removed completely at random, which is the easiest kind of missing data;
real irregular data often has structure, such as long gaps or more
observations when something changes. Finally, the depth-3 signature has
more features than there are training series on three of the four
datasets (16\,275 features for 180 series on NATOPS), which is workable
only because the classifier is regularised. For the neural models, the
clean results come from one seed, the settings were chosen on clean data
with small validation sets, and the LTC search is incomplete.

The next steps are the full grid with missing points for the Neural CDE
and LTC, which needs a faster implementation, and a dataset with
naturally irregular times.
